The family rhythm

Slow is the
secret ingredient.

A comfortable day with a baby is a successful day. These are the guardrails built into your plan.

01

One neighbourhood

Keep each half-day in one area. Use a taxi for the last kilometre when everyone is tired.

02

Reset after lunch

Plan a 45–90 minute pause for feeding, prayer, nappy changes or a proper nap.

03

Carrier + stroller

Bring both: stations and malls suit a stroller; shrines, stairs and crowds are easier with a carrier.

04

Warm, not bulky

Late November is cool. Pack layers, a rain cover and a warm pram blanket for Fuji mornings.

01

Transport

  • Tokyo trains generally stop around midnight.
  • Weekday rush hours are roughly 7–9 AM and 5–7 PM; avoid them with luggage and a stroller.
  • Use Yamato luggage forwarding between stays. Delivery timing and cutoff vary, so confirm at the counter rather than relying on a universal 4 PM rule.
  • If a future route arrives at KIX, reserve the airport express before travelling; this itinerary currently arrives at Narita.
02

Etiquette

  • Avoid walking while eating and keep voices low on public transport.
  • Do not tip.
  • Gesture with an open hand rather than pointing at people.
  • At many restaurants, take the bill to the front counter and place cash or a card in the payment tray.
03

Money & data

  • Carry a small coin pouch. Cards are common, but some businesses remain cash-only.
  • Keep a sensible arrival cash buffer rather than carrying the whole trip budget.
  • Set up an eSIM before departure and download offline maps.
  • Lunch sets can make higher-end meals more affordable.
04

Practical

  • Carry your passport when planning tax-free purchases.
  • From 1 November 2026, eligible purchases are paid tax-inclusive and refunded after departure checks; the old sealed-consumables rule is abolished.
  • Carry pocket tissues, hand towels and a small soap or sanitiser.
  • Tokyo Disney pricing varies by date—check the official calendar rather than budgeting from an old US$50 estimate.
